## Notes

### The truncation decision (ADR-0025) — the CP's one real design call

**Surfaced, never screened.** A length-terminated episode is a real
trajectory: engine-sampled ids, exact mask, captured logprobs up to the
cut, every gate green. The repo had in fact already decided this twice
without saying so — TR1's allowlist has admitted tail `length` since
CP-02 (the spec's own bullet scopes the tripwire to *aborts*, and
`length` at the tail is not one), and both bridges label the case
TRUNCATED-but-trainable (verl's comment: "CP-17 trained one live").
What CP-32 exposed was not a screening gap but an *invisibility* gap:
7/72 pooled thinking-on episodes hit the wall (max row 32,645/32,768 =
99.6%) and nothing anywhere said the number out loud.

Why not screen: qualification-time or `checks.py` rejection discards
real data at the one point nothing downstream can recover it (a
quarantined trace is gone for the trainer), which is this repo's
canonical failure shape pointed the other way; H-41's lesson is about
silent *absence* of evidence, not about policy-screening honestly
labeled data. The mid-chain case stays a hard S7 builder failure — there
the trace misrepresents the episode. Whether to train on TRUNCATED rows
is the trainer's policy, and the bridges already give the trainer the
label to exercise it.

What it prints, all three surfaces, unconditionally (zero is a
measurement too):

```
length-terminated: 1/2 accepted episodes ended finish_reason=length —
qualified by design; training on them is the trainer's call (ADR-0025)
```

(cli submit — test-proven at 1/2 doctored and 0/1 clean), plus
train.py's `[train] length-terminated: K/N …` at collect and at grade,
where the bridge's TRUNCATED count is aggregated beside it. The spec
carries the posture as an `[added at CP-33]` bullet beside TR1's
allowlist. No `checks.py` rule → no CP-34 stop; a future fail-closed
screen is priced as a `CheckPolicy` knob with its own allowance ADR.
