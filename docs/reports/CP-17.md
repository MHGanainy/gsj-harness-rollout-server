   the live argv names the checkpoint dir with all four legs unchanged.
2. **The deterministic probe** (`docs/polar/h200-loop/sync_proof.txt`) —
   the decisive one. Control first: two probes over the same 5783-id
   stream on **identical** weights →
   `mean|Δ|=0.000000 max|Δ|=0.000000 nonzero=0/5782`. The instrument has
   exactly zero noise on this engine (re-confirming CP-09′'s
   bit-determinism finding). Then across the sync →
   `mean|Δ|=0.041835 max|Δ|=5.829074 nonzero=5623/5782`. With a zero-noise
   instrument, all 5623 moved positions attribute to the weight update.
3. **The weights themselves** (`weight_delta.txt`): 263 of 310 shared
   tensors differ, `max |Δw| = 1.526e-05` — the scale one Adam step at
   lr 1e-5 should produce. (`lm_head.weight` is absent from the export

**A-13 (the drain rule) meets a real sync — what actually happened.** It
was satisfied **by construction, not by machinery**: the loop is
serialized, so at the sync boundary there were zero episode containers on
`gsj-staging-net` and the engine reported `Running: 0 reqs, Waiting: 0

rule can be honoured under concurrency. A real run overlaps collection
with training, and then the rule needs either an actual drain barrier or
P3's per-turn stamping to detect a session that spanned the sync. One
serialized sync exercises the *situation*, not the *mechanism* — recorded
as such.

**Does the loop close? YES, WITH FINDINGS.**

Every step is evidenced: **collect** (28 submissions, 27 qualifying, gates
green, cutoff held) → **convert** (27 real slime Samples, three assertions
live, `checks` trainer-side from the packaged pins) → **train** (one

| finding | cost | owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **F-08** — the vendored LOO post-processor divides by `stdev + 1e-6`; degenerate variance (the normal case at demo scale) explodes one advantage to 1e6. | **A-13 at concurrency** — the drain was satisfied by ordering, not by mechanism | a concurrent run needs a real barrier or P3 stamping live | **trainer** (P3 is carried and inert) |

**What the conversion to ADOPT means**: the architecture *and* the outcome
now have evidence. Polar owns the episode-execution layer, our shell stayed
thin, the H200 golden pair passed (condition 1), and traces produced
through this server have driven a real optimizer step in a real trainer and
come back as an updated policy that demonstrably serves the next
collection. A-6 — open since CP-00 — is resolved.

**What it does not mean, stated plainly**: one loop at 0.6B on 27 episodes
is not a training result. Nothing here shows learning, and the CP-17 design
deliberately does not test for it. Untested: multi-step training, any
concurrency between collection and training, weight sync at cadence, more
than one prompt in the group, and any model larger than 0.6B.

**What would still reverse the adopt** (narrowed, not eliminated): a
re-vendor that breaks the patch posture — the pending `prefix_merging`
refactor remains the named risk (A-8, untested; no second vendor has been
executed). The slime-bridge door is now **closed by measurement**: the
bridge is usable, and the frictions found are worked around at trainer-side
cost, not at Path C cost.

### What a real training run would need that this loop did not

1. **A weight-sync mechanism that does not restart the engine** — NCCL into
   trainer-owned engines, which means re-qualifying the estate against
   SGLang (the four legs, the pins, and the golden pair are all vLLM-shaped).
2. **P3's stamping live**, the moment collection and training overlap; and
   with it a real drain barrier rather than a serial ordering.
3. **A reward with signal at scale.** 1 in 27 at 0.6B makes a defensible
   single step and would make a hopeless training curve; either a stronger
   actor or a denser grader.
4. **A fix or a guard for F-08** before any multi-step run — an epsilon
   floor on a degenerate variance is a divergence waiting for a run long
   enough to find it.
5. **Collection throughput**: ~36 episodes took the better part of an hour
   serially through one engine. A real batch cadence needs parallel
   submission, which is exactly where the async/callback path (CP-08) and
   A-13's drain rule start interacting for real.
6. **Somewhere to put checkpoints.** The loop wrote 55 GB of image and ~2 GB
   of checkpoints for one step; retention, cleanup and checkpoint identity
   are the trainer's, and are entirely unbuilt.
