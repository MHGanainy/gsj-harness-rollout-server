**The parts not on the list** (the interesting ones):
1. **The silent clamp** — `thinking: "on"` is accepted by config and
   silently means OFF on the wire. Found at desk, before it cost the
   measurement.
2. **The reasoning round-trip field dance** — vLLM 0.26's qwen3 parser
   returns reasoning as `message.reasoning` (not `reasoning_content`); pi
   stores the field name as the block's signature and echoes history back
   under the same name; the engine maps inbound `reasoning` into the
   template's `reasoning_content` path. Probed: `reasoning`,
   `reasoning_content`, and a literal in-content `<think>` block all
   render identically (76 tokens vs 46 bare). Three parties, three
   spellings, one render — it works, but it is load-bearing coupling a
   re-vendor could snap; the visible symptom would be chain splits, which
   the receiver already rejects.
3. **The engine has a second thinking switch** — the serve argv's
   `--default-chat-template-kwargs '{"enable_thinking": false}'` (and
   `--reasoning-parser qwen3`, already there since CP-04′). Because pi
   sends the kwarg on every request, the default is dead weight under pi —
   but any client that omits the kwarg gets the argv's OFF. The four legs
   never move when the flag flips; that is what made "everything else
   identical" literally true (same engine PID served both legs).
4. **The re-pin geometry** — the 3-id thinking-on tail `[151644, 77091,
   198]` is NOT an `endswith`-suffix of the 7-id empty-think tail (which
   ends `[271, 151668, 271]`). So pinning the 3-id tail ALONE is
   **mode-asserting**, not the weakened template-integrity gate CP-23's
   re-pin description assumed — only pinning both tails weakens. Recorded
   in the spec §G6.

between OFF's 1/15 deliverables and ON's 8/15: thinking gets the 0.6B
model through the write step it otherwise abandons. The deliverables
themselves stay skeletal either way (63–350 bytes, several citation-free,
one generically hallucinated) — thinking fixes workflow completion, not
factual grounding, at this scale.

## Step 4 — are reasoning tokens trainable?

The mechanics first: the mask cannot say. Upstream Polar emits
`loss_mask = [1] * len(response_ids)` per sampled completion
(`record_utils.py:149`); interstitials get 0s; there is no third value
and no reasoning annotation (A-7/D4: reasoning masking was fork-only —
TR2 stays the canary that a re-vendor hasn't changed that). **"Don't
train on reasoning" is not expressible in the shipped trace.** It IS
expressible consumer-side, deterministically and tokenizer-free: the
`<think>`/`</think>` ids (151667/151668) segment the mask-1 spans — the
15-line `spans`/`think_segments` walk in `docs/polar/thinking/analyze.py`
is the whole algorithm. A consumer that wants a reasoning submask derives
it from ids; the builder's mask stays binary sampled-vs-interstitial.

Per regime:
- **OPD — yes, and it is the point.** The teacher scores every sampled
  token; distilling the reasoning *process* is the argument for thinking
  in the first place. The 67% think share is signal here, not noise.
- **SFT — no, at 0.6B, on its own reasoning.** Supervising on these
  traces means teaching the model to restate prompts in circles; the
  quality read above is the evidence. An SFT consumer must either derive
  the think submask and zero it, or take reasoning from a stronger
  teacher. Circularity is not a mask problem — it is a data problem.
- **RLVR — yes, with eyes open.** Reward lands on all mask-1 tokens;
  3/15 ON episodes would earn nonzero citation reward vs 1/15 OFF — more
  signal for GRPO/verl — and reasoning tokens legitimately share credit.
  The open eye: a median 67% of the gradient mass rides think tokens.

## Step 5 — the verdict

**GO.** At 0.6B on this task family, thinking costs ~2.7× wall clock and
~2× tokens, and buys: deliverable completion up **1/15 → 8/15**,
MCP retrieval successes up 2.5 → 4.1 per episode, rewardable episodes up
1/15 → 3/15, zero context deaths, zero infrastructure breakage —
`chains_total == 1` on every episode (A-22's cure held), every non-G6
gate silent, and G6 failing loudly on exactly and only the thing it pins.
The failure surface the CP existed to find did not appear; the measured
loop-and-ramble risk (CP-04's ghost) did not materialize under the pinned
sampling. What it commits Phase C to:

- **G6: re-pin, as per-mode pin data** (of CP-23's three options). The
  evidence: all 41 thinking-on turn openings end `[151644, 77091, 198]`;
  the empty tail appeared zero times. The measured correction: the 3-id
  tail alone is mode-asserting (it is not a suffix of the 7-id tail), so
  a thinking-on estate that pins ONLY it keeps a real gate — no rule
  reshaping, no `checks.py` line, ADR-0021's 528/528 untouched. C-2
  lands it, plus the estate discipline that `harness.thinking` and the
  pin agree (a mismatch fails every episode loudly — the correct
  failure).
- **Pins that re-derive: one** — `g6_expected_tail_ids`, per mode.
  Everything else measured unmoved: `chat_template_hash` (same file,
  same sha), `system_prompt_hash` (30/30 episodes), tokenizer/settings/
  roster/skill-card hashes, the genconfig.
- **Config surface**: `harness.thinking` becomes a validated knob —
  enumerate pi's levels (`off|minimal|low|medium|high|xhigh|max`),
  reject everything else, and say in the comment that all non-off levels
  are wire-equivalent under `qwen-chat-template`. The silent clamp
  (`"on"` → off) is the trap the validator exists to close.
- **Trainability**: OPD and RLVR train on the traces as shipped; SFT
  does not (at this scale) without a derived think submask or a teacher.
