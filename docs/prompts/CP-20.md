## Step 1 — Decide the route first
Two paths, genuinely different in cost, and the decision belongs at the
start rather than after code exists.

**Direct** — `Trace` → `DataProto` yourself. verl wants `input_ids`,
`attention_mask`, `position_ids`, `loss_mask`, `old_log_probs`,
`advantages`, batched `[B, L]` with padding. CP-16's bridge is the
template for everything except the target class.

**Via uni-agent** — reuse its trainer-side path (its plumbing into
`DataProto`) and feed it Polar traces instead of its own Framework's.
Polar's `Trafrom the uni-agent tensor contract
because §3.2 *was* that contract. Cheaper if it fits; and the discipline
that keeps it honest is absolute: **uni-agent's capture layer stays out** —
no codec, no gateway, no Framework, no sandbox providers. Only the segment
between "here is a trajectory" and "here is a verl batch."

Investigate before choosing. Read uni-agent's trainer-side entry point,
find what it hands verl, and determine whether it can be fed
externally-produced trajectories at all. **If it can't, say so plainly** —
that is a useful finding either way, and it settles a question that has
been open since the predecessor.

Record the choice with its reasoning in an external ADR.

## Step 2 — The conversion
Per the chosen route. The shape differences from slime that matter:

- **Batching.** slime takes per-sample objects; verl wants `[B, L]`
  tensors. So padding, stacking, and deriving `attention_mask` and
  `position_ids` from lengths — none of which Polar stores.
- **The prompt/response split.*`DataProto` conventions about
  where the prompt ends and the response begins are its own; get them from
  verl's source rather than by analogy to slime.
- **Advantages.** slime's bridge does leave-one-out; verl has its own
  estimators. Decide what the bridge produces — raw rewards and let verl
  compute, or advantages directly — and say why.
- **Status → maskability.** An ERROR trajectory must contribute zero
  gradient. Find verl's mechanism and use it; do not invent one.

## Step 3 — The three assertions, again
Non-negotiable, same as CP-16, each with a test that fails when removed:

1. **Mask before ratio** — interstitials carry `0.0` ≡ probability 1.0.
   verl computes importance ratios internally, so this is *more* dangerous
   here than in slime: assert the discipline at ingest, before verl ever
   sees the array.
2. **Sentinel rejection** — `-9999.0` at a trainable position, rejected at
   the bridge's own floor, independent of `CheckPolicy`.
3. **`checks` runs trainer-side** — the ession_result`,
   on what actually arrived, called and proven called.

Plus CP-09′'s floor if any replay-style validation appears: the measured
`0.008` / `0.21`, not the CP-18 anchor.

## Step 4 — What CP-16 taught, applied
The slime bridge's "demo-purpose" list is a checklist. For each item, say
what this bridge does instead — or that it inherits the same limitation
and why:

zero value-level logprob validation · interstitial trust by comment ·
pydantic-model-only inputs (ours takes callback JSON) · hardcoded
placeholder tokens · silent `0.0` rewards · single-attempt submits ·
string-literal status matching · positional group↔result pairing ·
oversize traces dropped wholesale.

And F-08 specifically: slime's vendored LOO post-processor divides by
`stdev + 1e-6`, so degenerate variance explodes one advantage to 1e6.
**Does verl's estimator have the same shape?** Read it. If it does, that
is a finding worth more than the bridge.

## Step 5 — Tests, fixture-driven
The real CP-09′ and CP-0s CP-16 used them.

- a real trace converts to a well-formed batch; shapes, lengths, and the
