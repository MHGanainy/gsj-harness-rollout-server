## Step 1 — ADR: does qualification screen truncation?
Row 30's harder half, and the CP-32 finding with real consequences:
**7 of 72 thinking-on episodes ended `finish_reason: length` at up to
99.6% of the window, qualified, and entered the batch silently.**

Decide, with reasoning, and record:

- **Is a length-terminated episode trainable?** It is a real trajectory
  the policy produced; it is also one whose final turn was cut off
  mid-thought. CP-16's bridge maps it to TRUNCATED and CP-21 trainey. So the answer may be yes-but-visibly rather than no.
- **Who screens it, if anyone?** Options, and they differ in kind:
  collection-time (the trainer's standard, like H-41), a `checks.py`
  rule (fail-closed, but `checks.py` is frozen and at its ceiling), the
  bridge (already knows — it computes TRUNCATED), or nothing but a
  printed count so a consumer sees it.
- **What CP-32 measured**: zero at n=15 serial, 7/72 pooled thinking-on.
  So this is a scale-and-mode property, not a constant.

**Recommendation: surface, don't screen** — a printed count at collect
and at grade, plus the bridge's existing TRUNCATED status made visible.
Screening at qualification would silently discard real trajectories, and
a `checks.py` rule would reject them at the receiver where nothing can
recover them. But make the call yourself and say why.

If the decision needs a `checks.py` rule, **stop and record it** — that
is CP-34 with its own allowance ADR.
