## Step 3 — The measurement
Per collection, and per episode where it matters:

- **Reasoning length** — tokens spent inside `<think>` blocks, absolute
  and as a fraction of the response. Distribution, not just a mean.
- **Context consumption** — total response length, how close to the 32k
  window, how many episodes hit `finish_reason: length`.
- **Tool use** — does it degrade? CP-04 measured greedy `<tool_call>`
  loops at this scale; the concern is that a reasoning budget gets spent
  looping. Compare tool execution counts, successful built-ins, and the
  `mcp_gsj_*` calls.
- **Quality, honestly** — read a handful of reasoning traces. Is the
  reasoning doing work, or is it restating the prompt? You cannot measure
  this rigorously at n=15 and shouldn't pretend to; report what you saw
  with examples and label it as judgement.
- **Cost** — wall clock per episode, and the qualification rate.

## Step 4 — Are reasoning tokens trainable?
Not a measurement — a decision that needs stating per regime:

- **OPD** — the teacher scores every token including reasoning. Distilling
  the reasoning process is arguably the point.
- **SFT** — supervising on the model's own reasoning is circular unless
  the reasoning is good, which is Step 3's judgement call.
- **RLVR** — reward lands on tokens including reasoning; fine in
  principle.

And the mechanical question underneath: **can a consumer even choose?**
The mask is the builder's, derived from what the engine sampled. If
reasoning tokens are indistinguishable from response tokens in the mask,
then "don't train on reasoning" isn't expressible — say so if true, and
say what it would take.
