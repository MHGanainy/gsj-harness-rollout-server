The correspondence between Greta Grunewald and the Greif Cooperative shows 23 letters exchanged before the May deadline. During the June inspection at the Duisburg yard, Greta Grunewald recorded that the cold-storage units had been moved to the customs office. According to the minutes kept by Bruno Neuhaus, the Greif Cooperative raised no objection to the proposed warehouse-lease schedule. A clerk noted in the margin that the cold-storage units were counted twice, once by Dominik Immelmann and once by a representative of Amber Logistics GmbH. During the February inspection at the Altona depot, Katrin Hofstetter recorded that the sealed ledgers had been moved to the loading bay.

The clerk's antechamber at the Duisburg yard was opened on a Thursday in the presence of Dominik Immelmann and Greta Grunewald. Jonas Dahlmann advised the Greif Cooperative in writing to postpone the warehouse-lease settlement until the sealed ledgers were re-inventoried. Cobalt Freight AG retained the Greif Cooperative to appraise the cold-storage units, an assignment expected to take 24 weeks.

Witnesses at the Altona depot recalled that Greta Grunewald and Dominik Immelmann disagreed about the warehouse-lease invoice on a Wednesday. An undated note, later attributed to Greta Grunewald, places the sealed ledgers in the archive cellar before the December audit. During the September inspection at the Altona depot, Jonas Dahlmann recorded that the cold-storage units had been moved to the records room. Cobalt Freight AG scheduled the follow-up review of the warehouse-lease file for the first Thursday of March. Bruno Neuhaus circulated a memorandum on the warehouse-lease file, and Cobalt Freight AG acknowledged receipt within 20 days.
